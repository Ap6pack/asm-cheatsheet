# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We want this project to be a place where anyone can learn attack surface management — whether you are writing your first `nmap` command or running an enterprise ASM program.

We pledge to make participation a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of experience level, background, identity, or any other personal characteristic.

## Our standards

Behaviour that builds this community:

- **Assume good faith.** Most disagreements are misunderstandings.
- **Be patient with beginners.** Everyone was new once, and "obvious" questions usually mean our documentation was unclear.
- **Give specific, actionable feedback.** "This command fails on macOS because `-r` isn't supported by BSD sed" beats "this is wrong."
- **Credit others' work.** Cite sources, link to upstream tools, and acknowledge who found what.
- **Accept correction gracefully.** Being wrong in public is how technical communities improve.

Behaviour that is not acceptable:

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks
- Discriminatory language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention
- Publishing others' private information without permission
- Gatekeeping — dismissing people for not already knowing something
- Deliberately posting harmful, destructive, or misleading commands
- Sustained disruption of discussions or reviews

## Security-specific expectations

This project deals with offensive security techniques, which raises the bar in two ways:

1. **Contributions must respect authorization boundaries.** Content, examples, and scripts must be framed for use against systems the reader owns or is authorized to test. Do not contribute material whose primary purpose is attacking third parties.
2. **Do not use this project to coordinate or promote unauthorized activity.** Issues, pull requests, and discussions are not a place to solicit attacks, share access to systems you do not own, or trade in stolen data.

Findings about *this project* should go through [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md), not a public issue.

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces — the repository, issues, pull requests, discussions, and any space where someone is representing the project.

## Enforcement

Report unacceptable behaviour to the maintainer via the contact on the [maintainer's GitHub profile](https://github.com/Ap6pack), or by opening a private security advisory if the report is sensitive.

All reports will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly. The maintainer is obliged to respect the privacy and security of the reporter.

Maintainers may take any action they deem appropriate, including warning, temporary ban, or permanent ban from project spaces. Maintainers who do not follow or enforce this Code of Conduct in good faith may face consequences determined by other maintainers.
